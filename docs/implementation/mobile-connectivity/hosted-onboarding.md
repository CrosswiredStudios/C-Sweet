# Hosted onboarding and phone access

These four deliveries are the first implementation sequence. Hosted uses the C-Sweet Marketplace for accounts, the directory for discovery, and the official relay for remote access. Headquarters runs on the operator's machine. Private starts with the existing local administrator flow.

Use the same named installation throughout the walkthroughs, for example **Northstar Headquarters**. Record each gate in the [acceptance register](acceptance-and-operations.md). The [identity guide](identity-and-installation-linking.md) specifies the linking protocol.

## H1 · Choose Hosted or Private on first run

**Deliverable.** A Headquarters build whose first registration screen offers **Hosted** and **Private** before asking for credentials. Start with IL-08 and the upgrade case in IL-13. Entering Hosted explains that the account lives on the C-Sweet Marketplace and offers **Create account** and **Sign in**. Private opens the current local form. Review H1 with linking disabled (`Installation:LinkingEnabled` off hides Hosted). Enable linking on designated pilot installations for the H2 live walkthrough before general rollout.

- `Register.razor`: Choose → Hosted or Private steps. With linking off, the page opens the Private form directly, exactly as before. Hosted shows **Create account** and **Sign in** disabled with a "not available in this version yet" notice until H2. Strings in all ten languages (Auth area).

## H2 · Register or sign in at the live Marketplace and finish HQ setup

**Deliverable.** Hosted setup uses the production Market account site, with sign-in served from the dedicated account hostname (proposed `https://account.c-sweet.com`, decision D7). Create account opens Market registration and Sign in opens Market login, each preserving the pending installation approval through authentication and email verification. Market handles the account credentials. HQ shows the confirmed account and installation, provisions its local administrator and recovery method, and continues the normal setup wizard. Use IL-01 through IL-05 and IL-09/10. Take the initial-owner identity link here; general member invitations are separate work.

**Owner walkthrough.** On fresh HQ A choose Hosted → Create account, verify email received in a real mailbox, compare the displayed device code, approve the named HQ, save local recovery codes and finish setup. On fresh HQ B choose Hosted → Sign in with the same existing account. Complete setup and confirm the original Market user ID and entitlements are preserved, with two distinct installation IDs. Repeat a returning-account run with an existing Market session and verify the approval screen identifies the account being used.

**Failure and recovery.** Deny or expire the approval before commit and retry or choose Private. Interrupt setup after the local commit and after remote registration but before acknowledgement. Resume once with the original installation key and verify one local owner, one remote installation per HQ and recoverable local sign-in. A Market outage produces a retryable Hosted state. Switching to Private before commit explicitly cancels the pending attempt.

## H3 · Sign in on a phone and see My Headquarters

**Deliverable.** A signed app offers **Sign in with C-Sweet** in the system browser. Use IL-07 and the minimal entry/chooser from the shell backlog. The authenticated directory lookup returns each completed HQ owned by this Market account exactly once with its stable installation ID and display name. Reuse IL-06/12 for the owner projection. A fresh phone can discover these instances without a QR code or manual server address.

**Publication boundary.** On setup completion, persist the setup transition and owner-publication outbox record together. If registration is still pending, reconcile it first. Bind the initial directory row to the installation's authenticated Market owner. Use a revisioned upsert and tombstone, retry after outages, and reconcile completed linked installations on restart. Until a route is ready, show the discovered instance with connection pending or unavailable. The directory uses the authenticated stable account identity; email is the familiar login name and can change.

## H4 · Select an instance and connect over cellular

**Deliverable.** Selecting a discovered HQ obtains a short-lived enrollment ticket scoped to that installation and phone key. The phone verifies the HQ pin, proves its key, completes the configured trusted approval, and opens one authorized, live read, such as the list of businesses. Implement the transport and dispatcher-entry spikes, relay routing, device lifecycle, the request tunnel with one marked route, and the business list rendered by that Headquarters' own web app in the thin shell ([tunnel guide](tunnel-and-thin-shell.md): TS-01 to TS-04, TS-06, TS-10, TS-14 to TS-16). If the TS-13 prototype failed, the business list is a bundled screen instead (TS-F1). Member rollout and push have their own gates.

**Implementation status (2026-11-14) — transport core (P0-07 rows a–c, e, f).**

- **Relay module in ConnectionService (`CSweet.ConnectionService.MobileRelay`), off unless `MobileRelay:Enabled`.** SignalR hub `/hubs/mobile/v1`, WebSockets only, MessagePack. Operator-provisioned routes name the Headquarters connector key. Headquarters attaches by signing a single-use relay challenge (`csweet-relay-attach\n{route}\n{nonce}`). A phone opens a channel with an admission ticket signed by the route key (`csweet-relay-ticket\n{route}\n{phone key thumbprint}\n{expires}`, at most five minutes) plus a signature from the phone key over a fresh challenge, so a stolen ticket is useless. Frames are opaque, at most 32 KiB, never logged; only the two parties can use a channel; at most two channels per phone and 64 per route; a new connector replaces the old one and closes its channels; disconnects close channels. 8 tests; full ConnectionService suite 353 of 353.
- **Transport library in C-Sweet (`CSweet.Mobile.Transport`).** Bounded duplex stream over relay frames with backpressure; pinned TLS (TLS 1.2+, SHA-256 SPKI pins, certificate validity, no web PKI); `HeadquartersRelayConnector` (TLS server, requires the phone client key to equal the key the relay admitted and to be enrolled; re-attaches after reconnect) and `PhoneRelaySession` (TLS client pinned to Headquarters, presenting the phone certificate). The phone's device key is both its relay admission key and its TLS client key. 10 tests: 1 MiB echo, wrong Headquarters pin, unenrolled phone, mismatched client key, dropped/duplicated/corrupted frame ends the session and a fresh channel works, buffer backpressure.
- **End to end through the real relay (container only, not committed):** TLS 1.3 negotiated over WebSockets/MessagePack; 512 KiB echoed; the relay inspected 68 frames and never saw the seeded plaintext marker.
- **Still open in P0-07:** row d (Headquarters on Windows with a key loaded from disk), row g (Android phone as client), row h (iPhone, Release/AOT MessagePack). Note: on Linux the client checks the pin just after the TLS handshake, so Headquarters may briefly see a session that the phone then drops; the phone sends nothing over it.
- **Tickets come from the C-Sweet directory (decided 2026-11-14).** A fresh phone cannot reach Headquarters to ask for one.

**Implementation status (2026-11-14) — Connect, first end-to-end slice.**

- **Market:** Headquarters publishes its relay binding (`PUT /api/installations/{id}/relay`: relay origin, route, inner TLS pin; signed by the installation key over `csweet-installation-relay\n{id:D}\n{origin}\n{route}\n{pin}\n{revision}\n{timestamp}`; higher revision wins). An Active member's app asks `POST /api/directory/installations/{id}/ticket` with its phone key and gets a five-minute ticket signed by the Market relay ticket key (`csweet-relay-ticket-directory\n{route}\n{installation}\n{phone thumbprint}\n{expires}`) plus relay origin, route and pin. Non-members get 404; `relay_not_configured` until Headquarters publishes. Public key at `/api/directory/ticket-key`. Migration `AddInstallationRelayBinding`. Market suite 33 of 33.
- **Relay:** accepts directory tickets only for routes the operator bound to the same installation; relay-only hosting mode for a dedicated deployment (`MobileRelay:RelayOnly`), HTTPS required outside Development. `compose.relay.yml` and `docs/mobile-relay.md` in ConnectionService. Suite 355 of 355.
- **Headquarters:** `MobileRelayConnectorService` (off unless `MobileRelay:Enabled`): the installation key doubles as the route key; a separate TLS key is created on first use and stored protected on the installation link (migration `AddInstallationLinkTlsKey`, hand-written). Publishes the relay binding, attaches, and answers each phone with a welcome naming the Headquarters and saying enrollment is required. No business data crosses yet; device enrollment and approval are the next slice.
- **App:** a per-install device key in protected storage (relay admission and TLS client key); **Connect** on the pending screen requests a ticket, opens the relay channel and pinned TLS, and shows "Connected securely to … (TLS 1.3)" or a specific failure (not turned on, no access, unreachable, secure connection failed, refused). 24 shell tests.
- **Test path:** local first (Market AppHost, Headquarters, relay on `localhost:5190`, `adb reverse` for 5163 and 5190), then the Mac Studio relay once the production Market has the issuer and ticket key enabled. Steps in ConnectionService `docs/mobile-relay.md`.

Stop the connector; the instance remains discoverable and shows unavailable. Restart, reconnect, then revoke that phone in HQ. Its next read fails while the local browser and the other HQ continue working. Repeat on both platforms.
