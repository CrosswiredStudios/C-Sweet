## From approved brief to first work

1. **Approve the outcomes.** Capture player outcomes, constraints, and non-goals in a ledger with stable keys, exact source revisions, and the approved wording. Each entry records its author. Naomi writes the experience outcomes, the creative non-goals and, by default, the feature-level outcomes. Marcus supplies the business constraints from the CEO's direction. When a specialist designer joins, that designer takes over the feature outcomes in their area and Naomi reviews them for vision. The designer proposes revisions to those outcomes; each previously approved revision keeps its original author. Gabriel submits the planning package and Marcus accepts it through the existing handoff. A later change to an approved obligation goes to the appropriate authority.
2. **Publish the Epic roadmap.** Gabriel and Victor group the outcomes into Epics, identify dependencies and open questions, and select the first increment. A future outcome can have an Epic as its owner until work approaches execution. Constraints and non-goals apply wherever relevant. Gabriel can split, regroup, add, or reorder future Stories within approved scope and retain their history. Outcome ownership on the board shows planned coverage. Completion requires evidence.
3. **Detail the first increment.** Publish only the Stories and executable tasks needed to deliver or test it. For classic Breakout, an engineer should be able to start once these tickets pass ordinary staffing, estimates, QA readiness, and preflight. The PoC passes when QA observes input, rebound, and brick removal in the runnable build. For a spike, name the question, the evidence to collect, and the decision the result will inform.
4. **Prepare the next likely increment during execution.** Start with one increment of lookahead. Gabriel may narrow or expand it when uncertainty, dependencies, and available capacity justify the cost. Victor can draft scoring while brick destruction is in development by recording what scoring assumes about a brick hit. Drafts confer no execution authority. Before scheduling, Gabriel checks the current plan revision, actual prerequisite results, capacity, and findings. Keep the portions of a draft whose assumptions still hold. Current delivery and its reviews have priority over speculative drafting.

QA receives each criterion's stable identity, approved wording, source, and required evidence alongside the refined task. A task may sharpen a criterion. QA still checks the original obligation against the actual candidate. Preserve the association between criteria and browser-interaction evidence through edits and reordering. A test that merely renders a paddle cannot satisfy a rebound criterion. The current game contract expresses interaction requirements by criterion position in `GameProposedWorkItemV1.InteractionCriteria`.

## How complexity drives staffing

Gabriel assesses staffing at kickoff, when approaching work needs a new capability, and at sprint review. He considers the uncertainty in the game rules, technical constraints, content volume, interaction between systems, and evidence needed for acceptance. For each gap he identifies the decision or deliverable, when it is needed, and whether the assigned team has the expertise and capacity. He can use the current team, request a bounded contribution from an available specialist, or propose hiring for sustained work. Code resolves actual role eligibility, roster, grants, and budget authority.

| Work in the brief | Staffing judgment to exercise |
|---|---|
| Familiar Breakout rules and one playable loop | Technical direction, engineering, and independent QA are the natural delivery capabilities. A brief that settles the rules can support the first build without a dedicated Designer. |
| Power-ups that interact with paddle size, speed, or scoring | Gabriel identifies the unresolved interaction and who should settle it. Existing rules may suffice. One tuning question may warrant a Game Designer consultation. Repeated balancing and playtest interpretation may justify ongoing design capacity. |
| A branching narrative with persistent consequences | An early Narrative Designer contribution may unlock the first branch. Victor can focus technical work on state persistence and replay. Bring in other capabilities as their deliverables approach. |

The existing Game Designer, Level Designer, Narrative Designer, Art Director, Audio Designer, UI/UX and Accessibility Designer, and Playtest Researcher can contribute within their specialties. Assigning a Designer should allow a scoped contribution to a Story while progressive planning continues. For a feel question, QA supplies observed behavior, a Designer or Naomi supplies an attributed design assessment, and the Playtest Researcher defines the protocol and interprets actual participant evidence where available. Every sprint review produces a feel summary for Marcus's CEO reporting: how to run the build, what to try, who observed or assessed what, and which conclusions are still hypotheses. A sprint that produces only research or a prerequisite reports what was learned and when a playable build is expected. The summary invites the CEO to play and never holds work by itself. After the PoC, scoring and other dependent work proceed unless Gabriel names a specific decision that needs the CEO (see the hold row below). Builds and updates are published internally and reported without stopping development. Public launch remains a separately reserved approval. [Studio role boundaries](game-studio-role-boundaries.md)

## Sprint review and second judgment

At **every sprint completion**, Gabriel compares the intended goal with the build or other deliverable, QA findings, unfinished work, draft assumptions, and management direction received since the previous review. A prerequisite sprint can close while the larger increment still awaits acceptance. Record what that sprint established and carry the open goal forward. A material finding, blocker, or new direction can also trigger an earlier review. Persist the decision once for the evidence reviewed and reread current state on duplicate or missed notifications. [Recorded prerequisite sprint](game-studio-production-review.md)

A routine review can be one short decision to proceed, citing the evidence and the next goal. Gabriel can combine actions, such as moving collision repair ahead of scoring and requesting a technical consultation. He chooses whom to consult for ordinary planning.

Each review records one decision with the next goal, actions from a fixed set (proceed, resequence, refine further, request a contribution or staffing, escalate, hold for the CEO), the evidence it cites, and what would prompt an earlier revisit. Code checks that every cited reference resolves to a board item, QA report, build, artifact or authenticated management decision, and that sequence changes keep approved scope and an acyclic dependency graph. An action that needs a second judgment is recorded as pending with its proposal and evidence, and takes effect only once the authorized receipt exists. The rest of the decision applies immediately: Gabriel can reorder approved work while a hire proposed in the same review is pending. This decision replaces the fixed outline order in `NextProgressiveStory` as the selector of successor work. That order remains the default suggestion behind proceed.

Code recognizes the following actions at the point where they take effect.

| Proposed action | Required judgment and effect |
|---|---|
| Hire or commit spend beyond delegated authority | Use the existing authorized staffing or spending decision for the exact proposal. That decision supplies the independent judgment. An experiment already covered by a delegation can proceed under its terms. |
| Dismiss a QA finding, defer a required check, or change acceptance obligations | Use the authorized finding disposition or scope amendment. Retain the original finding, its evidence, who decided, and what verification is still due. Gabriel can move a repair up the queue himself. Independent QA verifies the resulting candidate. |
| Hold otherwise ready work for the CEO | Gabriel identifies the specific decision that needs the CEO, the tickets that wait, and what can continue. Marcus confirms which tickets wait and sends the request when the decision is needed, independently of the CEO's regular reporting cadence. A feel summary or invitation to play never holds work. Silence leaves the decision open and independent work continues. |

## Implementation order and compatibility

1. **Establish the baseline and scope model.** Record milestones from the brief being ready for planning, and again from package acceptance onward. Bind the outcome ledger to that package and keep the mutable delivery sequence, dependencies, and refinement depth in a separately revisioned record. Let agents return judgments in bounded pieces. Code supplies identities, revisions, bindings, and authority. Treat twelve tasks as a guide for sizing model work and allow paging or splitting when the work calls for it. Give acceptance criteria a stable identity in the game contracts and key `InteractionCriteria` evidence by that identity in an additive field, keeping positional indexes for existing tickets.
2. **Prove delivery by selected Stories across the stack.** Victor's `OutlineIncrement` has one `StoryKey`. Gabriel's `DetailedIncrementEpicIds` and `HasAcceptedProgressiveIncrements` and the SDK's `HierarchicalProjectDelivery.PrepareIncrementAsync` use Epic membership. Exercise one Epic with two Stories in separate increments. Accept A, restart, then schedule and accept B. Only the selected work should enter each increment. Include a regression for the Software Product Manager's existing whole-project `PrepareAsync` path.
3. **Publish and refine progressively.** Enable Epic-level future coverage, provisional lookahead, and optional Story-specific Designer contributions through the normal Producer and Technical Director entry points. Replace the whole Designer branch in Gabriel's planning flow (`SpecialistAgent.cs`, about lines 760–809), not only its first gate: the `designer is null` progressive-planning condition, the separate whole-backlog Designer session, the wait for that session, the expected backlog artifact, and the second `designer is null` check before progressive planning advances. Assigning a Designer then adds a scoped Story contribution without delaying the first engineering ticket. Replace the playable-first wording in Victor's outline prompt (`ProgressiveOutlinePages.cs`) and Gabriel's refinement request (`ProgressivePlanning.cs`) so the first increment tests the assumption most likely to change the plan. Recheck the current sequence before a draft enters execution. A stale draft or replay must neither alter active work nor create a second ticket or sprint.
4. **Introduce sprint decisions and staffing assessment.** Carry review evidence, proposed actions, and the resulting authoritative receipts across callbacks. Route each review's feel summary into Marcus's CEO reporting, and deliver hold requests as soon as they are needed rather than on the reporting cadence. Persist pending second-judgment actions and apply them only with their authorized receipts. Validate startup, ordinary continuation, and changes after QA or management input. Use existing research-spike work for evidence increments. Evaluate parallel prototype branches separately after the pilot.

Use additive, optional game contracts for information exchanged between agents. Keep local planning state with its owner. Omit absent new fields from serialized payloads to preserve retained digests. Projects with an existing frozen outline continue under their original rules. Verify package consumers and release changes through published packages and Installed Agents. ## Breakout pilot and adoption decision

Compare the current and proposed flows with the same approved brief, starting roster, model settings, tools, and compute capacity for each paired scenario. Target three to five comparable runs per variant. Record individual timings, stalls, and incomplete runs so one unusually fast completion cannot hide a recurring failure.

| Scenario | Evidence to inspect |
|---|---|
| Classic Breakout, clean run | Future outcomes are visible as Epics. The engineer starts the PoC before later Stories are detailed. QA verifies the playable loop. Gabriel cites the evidence and proceeds to scoring without a routine extra review or a CEO hold. The PoC feel summary invites the CEO to play. |
| Designer already assigned | Gabriel publishes the Epic roadmap and asks the Designer for one scoped contribution. An unrelated ready PoC ticket starts while that contribution is pending. A Designer hired later can propose revisions to feature outcomes, and earlier approved revisions keep their authors. |
| Same brief, collision defect | Supply the same reproducible high-speed collision finding in both flows. In the proposed flow, surface it while scoring is still a draft. Check that Gabriel prioritizes the affected work, retains valid scoring detail, keeps the finding open through repair, and requires the relevant verification before acceptance. |
| Breakout with interacting power-ups | Change that one design constraint and compare Gabriel's reasoning and staffing proposal with classic Breakout. Existing coverage, a consultation, and a hire can each be sound if the evidence supports the choice. |
| Branching narrative, planning only | Before broader rollout, check that the agents identify a narrative or state-management risk, choose a defensible first deliverable, and justify the team for that brief. |

Measure elapsed time from the brief being ready for planning, and separately from package acceptance, to the first executable ticket, first engineer start, first independently verified playable build, and successor engineer start after acceptance. For a spike, record time to an evidence-backed answer as a separate milestone. Break down model generation, coordination waits, board publication, and delivery readiness. Count planning moved ahead of package acceptance toward startup, so earlier planning cannot pass for a faster start. Report time spent waiting on a CEO hold separately, and confirm each sprint's feel summary reached Marcus's CEO report. Track tokens per verified increment, executed work later discarded, and provisional drafts revised or discarded. At sprint level, report covered, verified, and outstanding outcomes. At release, check the evidence for every approved obligation.

Exercise management input and recovery alongside the pilot. Deliver a changed priority before successor scheduling, replay its notification, and restart Gabriel. Verify one recorded decision, a single successor, and continued independent work. Exercise the three second-judgment cases and confirm routine reprioritization still proceeds within Gabriel's authority. Restart Gabriel between proposing a hire and its approval. Verify that he neither submits a duplicate proposal nor treats the pending action as approved, and that approved reordering from the same review has already taken effect.

Adopt the workflow if comparable runs reach a verified playable build sooner and reduce the wait for successor work while preserving scope coverage, independent acceptance, and justified staffing decisions. Report speed, token cost, and discarded execution together. The historical thirty-minute replacement outline is a regression example for unnecessary regeneration. Typical startup and handoff costs must come from the pilot.
